What Reiki is

Reiki is a gentle, hands-on practice that began in Japan in the early 1920s with a teacher named Mikao Usui. The word roughly translates as “universal life energy.”

In a session, the practitioner rests their hands lightly on, or just above, different areas of the body for a few minutes at a time. There’s no pressure, massage or manipulation. Practitioners describe it as supporting the body’s own capacity to relax and rebalance.

You don’t need to share any particular belief about energy to come for a session. Plenty of people simply find it deeply restful.

What happens in a session

  • You stay fully clothed. Just take off your shoes. Comfortable clothes are best.
  • You lie on a padded table, face up, with pillows and a blanket. If lying flat isn’t comfortable, we can adjust or you can sit in a chair.
  • Touch is light, or none at all. Caroline’s hands rest gently on places like the head, shoulders, torso, knees and feet. If you’d prefer no touch, she holds her hands a few inches above the body instead. You can change your mind at any point.
  • It’s quiet. There may be soft music, or silence if you prefer. You don’t need to do anything. Talking is fine; so is falling asleep.
  • Afterward there’s time to sit up slowly, have some water and ask any questions.

What people often notice

Everyone’s experience is different, and there’s no right way for a session to feel. People commonly mention:

  • Feeling deeply relaxed, sometimes more than they have in a while
  • Warmth, gentle tingling or heaviness where hands are placed
  • A quieter mind, or drifting in and out of sleep
  • Feeling calmer or lighter for the rest of the day
  • Sleeping well that night

Some people notice nothing in particular during the session and simply feel rested afterward. That’s completely normal too.

What the research says

Honestly: research on Reiki is still limited, and studies tend to be small. Some suggest it may help with relaxation, anxiety, and a general sense of wellbeing. It has not been shown to treat or cure any disease or medical condition.

That’s why Reiki is offered here as what it is: a calm, caring, low-risk practice that many people find helpful, not a medical treatment. Some hospitals and cancer centers offer Reiki to patients on the same basis, as complementary care.

Reiki and medical care

Reiki complements medical care. It never replaces it. Caroline doesn’t diagnose conditions, prescribe, or advise on medication or treatment. Please keep seeing your doctor and following their advice. If you’re in active treatment, it’s a good idea to let your care team know you’re trying Reiki.

If you have a specific health concern about whether a session is right for you, mention it when you get in touch and Caroline will talk it through with you.
